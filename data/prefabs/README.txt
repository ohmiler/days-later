Building layouts, one file each. The city picks one that fits each plot.
Lay them out the way the real thing is in Bangkok: a shophouse (ตึกแถว) is
about 4 m wide and 13-15 m deep (a cell is about a metre), the shop at the
front behind a rolling steel shutter, the kitchen, bathroom and back door at
the back, the stairs up in between, and the bedrooms upstairs.

The first lines are settings (lines starting # are notes):
  kinds: shop            which buildings may use it (shop, store)
  signs: ร้านทอง, โรงรับจำนำ   the shop signs it is for (commas between);
                         leave it out for a plan any shop without its own plan
                         (and any home) may use
  widen: 3               for a plot wider than the plan, repeat this column
                         (a door, stairs or bed in it gets wall or floor beside
                         it instead of a twin); plots are up to 2 cells wider
Then the plan, as seen from above, back at the top and the street at the
bottom. Every row is the same width (the building's width). A row that
starts with ~ can be repeated to make the building deeper (the ~ is not part
of the row); the plan itself must be no deeper than the shallowest plot (13).

A line "floor 2:" starts the plan of the floor above: the same number of
rows, the same ~ rows, and the stairs S in the same place. Up there: walls W,
floor ., open doorways d, beds and furniture as below.

  W wall            . floor
  D front door (bottom row)          B back door (top row; a wall if nothing is behind)
  d door inside the building          w shop window in the front wall (sometimes wall)
  U rolling steel shutter (bottom row, two or more side by side: they go up and down together)
Furniture you can search (it blocks the cell):
  f fridge   c cabinet   s shelf (stocked like the shop)   k counter   t table   x crate
  g glass display counter   M barber's mirror and counter   X tool cabinet
  H food cart (dressed for the eatery's sign)   Z safe   F food cupboard (ตู้กับข้าว)
  K kitchen sink
  b bed (b with another b under it is a full-length bed)
  ? whatever the shop sells from (the shop's own furniture: shelves, fridges...)
Things on the floor (no blocking):
  S stairs (up to floor 2 if there is one, and on to the roof)
  m mattress   v tv   n fan   h spirit shrine   o boxes   p noodle pot
  r table and stools   i tyres   e motorbike   l oil stain
  q squat toilet   C curtain   j shoes at the door   I Buddha shelf high on the wall
Things too big to walk through:
  A barber's chair   G gas stove and tank   J water jar (โอ่ง)   O wooden sofa
  a Chinese altar   y sacks   E dress stand   L massage armchair   V clinic couch
  u hair-wash basin   z bench
Things with a state (see Things):
  T a tap (7 in 10 have one)   R a radio (about 1 in 5)

The room with the front door holds what the shop sells. If the family lives
there (a bed anywhere, usually upstairs), the other rooms hold what homes
hold; a layout without beds is all shop.
